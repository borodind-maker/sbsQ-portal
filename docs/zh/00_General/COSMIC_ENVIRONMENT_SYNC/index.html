    🌌 Cosmic Environment Sync: Solar & Lunar Resilience

    This document describes how the sbsQ system accounts for external cosmic and atmospheric factors to maintain swarm intelligence stability in dynamic environments.

    ☀️ 1. Solar Activity & Geomagnetic Storms (Kp-Index)

    Solar flares directly affect the planet's physics and the drones' electromagnetic senses.

    System Impact:

    • Schumann Resonance: During storms, the ionosphere lowers, causing "compression" of the resonator and shifting the base frequency from GHS-Planetary Resonance frequency to higher values.
    • GPS Integrity: Geomagnetic disturbances create ionospheric delays leading to positioning errors (drift up to 10-30 meters).
    • Radio Communication: "Dead zones" may occur in the Mesh network due to ionization.

    Adaptation Strategy (Reflex Layer):

    1. Dynamic Offset: SchumannClient receives Kp-index data and adjusts cached_offset. The swarm synchronizes to the planet's "accelerated" rhythm.
    2. Lo-Fi Navigation: During high Kp-index (>5), the system deprioritizes GPS in favor of Visual Odometry and Ternary Displacement.
    3. Safety Buffer: Increased safety distance between swarm nodes to prevent collisions during GPS jumps.

    2. Lunar Phases & Illumination Levels

    The Moon's phase determines the "visual bandwidth" of the environment during night operations.

    System Impact:

    • Computer Vision: Direct correlation between phase and accuracy of YOLO/SAM algorithms without active lighting.
    • Stealth Mode: Avoiding active LiDAR/IR use during high natural illumination (Full Moon).

    Adaptation Strategy:

    1. Sensor Fusion Bias:
    2. Full Moon (Illumination > 70%): Priority on Vision-based navigation.
    3. New Moon (Illumination < 20%): Automatic transition to Active Scanning (LiDAR/Sonic) and thermal channels.
    4. Night Flight Suitability: Risks are calculated by the MoonPhaseService.

    🛰 3. Starlink: Anti-Spoofing & Deep Watch

    The sbsQ system uses the Starlink constellation not just for communication, but as a "cosmic anchor" to verify the authenticity of navigation data.

    Security Impact:

    • GPS Anti-Spoofing: Attackers can spoof GPS signals, but they cannot spoof the motion physics of Starlink satellites.
    • Timing Advance (TA): The system analyzes the TA rate of change (LTE Timing Advance). LEO satellites (Starlink) move at over 27,000 km/h, creating a unique "signature" of Doppler shift and signal delay.

    Protection Strategy:

    1. Orbital Cross-Check: AntiSpoofingEngine compares visible Starlink satellites with calculated orbits (TLE data). If GPS says we are at point A, but the Starlink satellites above us are not the ones supposed to be there — a Critical Risk is logged.
    2. High-Speed Validator: If StarlinkAntispoofing detects a static TA (typical for ground spoofing towers), the system ignores GPS data and switches to inertial navigation.
